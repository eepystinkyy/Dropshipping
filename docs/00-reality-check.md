# 00 — Reality Check

Read this before spending a single dollar or hour. The goal here is to make sure we are
entering the *right* business, not a business that stopped existing 18 months ago.

## Your framing, corrected

You said the market is "over saturated." That's the common diagnosis and it's the wrong
one. Saturation implies too many sellers chasing the same demand — a competitive problem
you can out-work. That's not what happened.

What actually happened is that the **cost structure inverted**. Two inputs the model
depended on both went the wrong way at once, and neither is something effort fixes:

| Input | Then | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Import duty on a $20 parcel from China | $0 (de minimis) | Full duty + tariff; a $25 item lands at $40–60 |
| Meta CPM | ~$7.50 (2020) | $14.19 all-industry / $10.42 ecommerce, +20% YoY, +89% since 2020 |
| Delivery expectation | 2–4 weeks tolerated | 2–5 days (set by Amazon, Temu, Shein) |
| Required ROAS at 25% margin | achievable | ~4:1 to break even, vs ~2.19:1 global average |

The gap between "required 4:1" and "typical 2.19:1" is the whole story. You are not
competing against other dropshippers for attention. You are competing against
**arithmetic that no longer closes**.

Working "from head to toes" is exactly the right instinct — it's just that the thing to
work on isn't a better-executed version of the old model. It's a different model.

## What specifically is dead

Be blunt about it so we don't accidentally rebuild it:

- ❌ **AliExpress → direct-to-customer shipping.** Duty now applies per parcel with no
  economy of scale. 10–20 day delivery. This is the single most-taught model and it is
  the single most broken one.
- ❌ **"Winning product" arbitrage.** Finding a trending item and marking it up 3x. Ad
  costs eat the entire spread, and any product you can find, ten thousand others can too
  — by definition, since you're all using the same spy tools.
- ❌ **Cold paid traffic as the primary channel from day one.** At $14 CPM, ~1% CTR and
  ~1.4–2% conversion, blended CAC lands around $70. You cannot pay $70 to acquire a $35
  order. See [`01-unit-economics.md`](01-unit-economics.md).
- ❌ **General stores.** No audience, no content angle, no retention, nothing to
  optimise. Zero defensibility.

## What survives

- ✅ **Organic-content-led launch.** TikTok Shop did $20B+ GMV in 2025, growing ~150%
  YoY. Organic reach still exists there in a way it does not on Meta. Your first 100
  orders should cost approximately $0 in ad spend.
- ✅ **Higher AOV / considered purchase.** $60–150 baskets, built with bundles. The
  margin has to be big enough in absolute dollars to survive a real CAC.
- ✅ **Repeat-purchase categories.** Consumables, refills, replenishables. If order 2 and
  3 come at near-zero CAC, an unprofitable first order becomes a profitable customer.
  Target LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1.
- ✅ **Validate dropshipped, then hold inventory.** Use supplier fulfillment to prove
  demand cheaply, then bulk-import to a 3PL. Bulk import amortises duty across a
  container instead of paying it per parcel, and cuts delivery to 2–3 days. This is the
  step that converts a marginal business into a real one.
- ✅ **Domestic / regional suppliers.** Higher unit cost, but no customs exposure, fast
  shipping, easier returns. Often nets out better than a cheaper Chinese unit once duty
  and refund rates are counted.

## The honest odds

- Most attempts fail. That was true before the rule changes and is more true now.
- The ones that work look like **small brands**, not stores — one narrow audience, 1–5
  SKUs, a real content engine, and repeat customers.
- Realistic timeline to profitability: **6–12 months**, not 6 weeks.
- Realistic starting capital: **$2,000–5,000**, of which most goes to samples, content
  production, and the first inventory buy — *not* to ads.
- The scarce resource is not money or product ideas. It's **consistent content output**
  and **patience through the validation phase**.

## The decision this document forces

Given the above, we are explicitly **not** building "a dropshipping store." We are
building a **single-niche product brand with supplier-fulfilled validation**. Dropshipping
is a logistics choice for months 1–4, not the business model.

If that trade — slower, more work, more real — isn't the thing you want, this is the
cheapest possible moment to say so. Stopping here costs nothing. Stopping in month 4
costs a few thousand dollars and a lot of evenings.

If it is the thing you want, go to [`01-unit-economics.md`](01-unit-economics.md) and run
the numbers on a real product idea before doing anything else.
