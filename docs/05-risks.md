# 05 — Risk Register

Ranked by expected damage. The first three have killed more of these businesses than
everything else combined.

---

### 1. 🔴 Paid acquisition never becomes profitable

**Likelihood: high.** The core risk. At benchmark CAC (~$65) against typical contribution
($40–50), paid may simply never close — even in Scenario B it only reaches 1.6:1.

*Mitigation:* Treat organic as the primary channel indefinitely, not as a bridge. Build
LTV through repeat purchase so the second and third orders carry the CAC. Never scale
spend past LTV:CAC 3:1. Accept that this may be a $10–30k/year organic business rather
than a $500k paid-scaled one — that's still a real outcome, and it's the honest base case.

---

### 2. 🔴 Content engine stalls

**Likelihood: high.** The plan requires ~90 videos in 90 days. Most people stop around 20
when early videos get 300 views.

*Mitigation:* Batch-film weekly rather than daily. Pick an audience you actually care
about — this is precisely why audience-first selection matters. Judge by week-4 output,
not week-1 views. If you cannot sustain the output, this business does not work, and it's
better to know that in week 3 than month 6.

---

### 3. 🔴 Cash flow, not profit, kills it

**Likelihood: medium-high.** Phase 4's inventory buy consumes cash before it returns any.
Growth makes this *worse*, not better — a fast-growing store with 60-day inventory cover
and 2-day payouts can be profitable on paper and insolvent in the bank.

*Mitigation:* Start with 30–60 days of inventory cover, never more. Model the cash
conversion cycle before the first PO. Keep 3 months of fixed costs untouched. Do not
finance inventory with credit in year one.

---

### 4. 🟠 Payment processing revoked

**Likelihood: low-medium. Impact: total.** Chargebacks over 1% can end Shopify Payments /
Stripe access. Without processing there is no business, and reinstatement is rare.

*Mitigation:* Ruthless fulfillment SLAs. Real tracking on every order. Refund fast and
without argument on anything ambiguous. Keep a secondary processor onboarded before you
need it.

---

### 5. 🟠 Tariff / regulatory change

**Likelihood: medium.** De minimis is already gone and statutory repeal lands Jul 2027.
Rates can move again with little notice.

*Mitigation:* DDP quotes in writing. Re-run unit economics quarterly. Prefer suppliers in
more than one country. Move to held inventory early — bulk import is far less exposed to
per-parcel rule changes than direct shipping.

---

### 6. 🟠 Supplier failure

**Likelihood: medium.** Quality drift, stockouts, disappearance, or quietly substituting
a cheaper component after your samples were good.

*Mitigation:* Three qualified suppliers before launch. Inspect every bulk order via a
third party. Never let one supplier hold both your inventory and your only tooling.

---

### 7. 🟡 Product liability

**Likelihood: low. Impact: severe.** One injury claim can exceed everything the business
has earned.

*Mitigation:* Entity separation from day one. Avoid the excluded categories in
[`02-product-selection.md`](02-product-selection.md#hard-filters). Product liability
insurance before scaling. No health or safety claims in any copy, ever.

---

### 8. 🟡 Competitor copies the product

**Likelihood: high. Impact: low-moderate.** If it works, someone will copy it — the
product itself is never the moat.

*Mitigation:* The defensible assets are the audience, the content library, the email
list, and the brand. Build those deliberately. A copied product with no audience is not a
competitor.

---

## Pre-mortem

*It is 12 months from now and this failed. What happened?*

The most likely story, in order: you picked a product you had no personal connection to
because the margins looked good; content became a chore by week 5 and output dropped;
with no organic traction you turned on ads to force it; ads ran at 1.5:1 and you spent
$1,500 proving the maths in `01-unit-economics.md` was right; you concluded the market
was saturated and stopped.

**Every step of that is prevented by Gate 1 and Gate 3.** The gates only work if you
honour them when they're inconvenient — which is the only time they ever trigger.

## Stop-loss

Agree these now, in writing, while nothing is at stake:

- **Money:** total exposure capped at $5,000. No credit, no borrowing to continue.
- **Time:** if Gate 3 fails twice with different products, stop for 6 months.
- **Health:** if this is costing sleep or relationships, it's not worth it. There is no
  version of this business that justifies that price.
